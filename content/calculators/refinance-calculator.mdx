Refinancing replaces your current mortgage with a new one, usually to get
a lower rate, a lower payment or a different term. The monthly saving is
easy to see. What is harder to see is whether the refinance actually saves
money once closing costs and a reset loan term are counted. This calculator
compares the two loans side by side, finds the month your closing costs are
paid back, and totals what each path costs from today until the house is
paid off.

## How to Use This Refinance Calculator

Enter your current loan balance, its interest rate and how many years are
left on it. Then enter the rate and term you have been offered on the new
loan, and the total closing costs, including lender fees, appraisal, title
charges and any points. Turn on the toggle if you plan to roll those costs
into the new loan instead of paying them in cash.

The results compare principal-and-interest payments, show your monthly
saving and break-even point, and give the total interest left on each loan
plus the lifetime net savings after closing costs. Property tax and
insurance are left out because they are the same whichever loan you have.

## How the Comparison Is Calculated

```
Monthly payment        = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
Monthly savings        = Current payment − New payment
Break-even (months)    = Closing costs ÷ Monthly savings, rounded up
Lifetime net savings   = All remaining current payments
                         − (All new-loan payments + Cash closing costs)
```

**L** is the loan balance, **r** is the annual rate divided by 12, and
**n** is the number of monthly payments left. If you roll closing costs in,
they are added to **L** for the new loan and are not counted a second time
as cash. If the new payment is not lower, there is no monthly saving to pay
back the costs, so the calculator reports no break-even point.

## Example Calculation

You owe $300,000 at 7% with 25 years left. A lender offers 6% on a new
30-year loan with $6,000 in closing costs, which you pay in cash.

- Current payment: **$2,120.34** a month
- New payment: **$1,798.65** a month
- Monthly savings: **$321.69**
- Break-even: $6,000 ÷ $321.69 = 18.65, so **19 months**

That looks like an easy decision. But the new loan runs 30 years instead of
the 25 you had left. Over its remaining life the current loan costs
$636,102.00 in payments, including $336,101.28 of interest. The new loan
costs $647,514.00 in payments, including $347,514.57 of interest, plus the
$6,000 of closing costs, for $653,514.00. The refinance **costs $17,412.00
more** overall, even though the rate is a full point lower.

Now take the same 6% rate on a 20-year term:

| Option | Monthly payment | Total cost from today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep current loan | $2,120.34 | $636,102.00 |
| Refinance, 30 years | $1,798.65 | $653,514.00 |
| Refinance, 20 years | $2,149.29 | $521,829.60 |

The 20-year payment is $28.95 higher, so there is no monthly break-even,
yet it saves **$114,272.40** over the life of the loan because the balance
is paid off five years sooner than the original schedule.

## Why a Lower Payment Is Not the Same as Saving Money

A new 30-year loan restarts the amortization clock. Part of the lower
payment comes from the lower rate, and part comes from spreading the same
balance over more years. Those extra years add interest, and early payments
on any new mortgage are mostly interest rather than principal. If the term
extension outweighs the rate cut, you pay less each month but more in
total. The lifetime net savings figure exists to catch exactly this case.

A lower payment can still be the right call if you need the monthly cash
flow, or if you plan to make extra principal payments to stay on your
original payoff date. Just make it a deliberate choice.

## What Affects Whether Refinancing Pays Off

- **Rate difference** — the bigger the drop, the faster you break even.
- **Closing costs** — typically a few percent of the loan; larger costs
  push the break-even point further out.
- **How long you will keep the loan** — if you sell or refinance again
  before the break-even month, you lose money.
- **New term versus years remaining** — matching or shortening the term
  protects your lifetime savings.
- **Rolling in costs** — avoids cash today but adds interest for the full
  term.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Judging the deal by the monthly payment alone.** Compare total cost
  from today, not just the payment.
- **Resetting to 30 years without noticing.** If you are 5 years into a
  loan, a new 30-year term adds 5 years of payments.
- **Ignoring how long you will stay.** A 19-month break-even is no help if
  you move in a year.
- **Leaving closing costs out of the comparison.** Points, appraisal and
  title fees are real money whether paid in cash or rolled in.
- **Not shopping lenders.** Rates and fees differ, so compare Loan
  Estimates from several lenders on the same day.
