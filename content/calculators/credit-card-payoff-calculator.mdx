Credit card debt behaves differently from a mortgage or auto loan, and that
difference is exactly what makes minimum payments so dangerous. Because the
minimum is usually a percentage of your current balance, it shrinks as your
balance shrinks — so an ever-smaller share of each payment goes to principal,
and payoff can stretch out for decades.

## How to Use This Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Enter your current balance and APR, then choose whether you'll pay a fixed
amount each month or the card's minimum payment. For a fixed payment, enter
the amount; for the minimum, enter the percentage and dollar floor your card
uses (check a recent statement — 2% of the balance with a $25 floor is
typical). If you expect to keep charging the card, add an estimated monthly
purchase amount to see its effect on payoff time.

## The Minimum-Payment Trap

A fixed payment stays the same every month, so as your balance falls, a
larger and larger share of it goes to principal — payoff accelerates. A
minimum payment does the opposite: it's recalculated from the current
balance each month, so it shrinks right along with your progress, and an
ever-smaller share goes to principal. On a high-rate balance, this can mean
the payoff timeline stretches past what most people would expect from looking
at the balance alone.

## How This Calculator Determines If Payoff Is Even Possible

Each month, interest is calculated on the current balance and added to it,
then the payment is applied. If the payment doesn't exceed that month's
interest, the balance cannot decrease — it can only grow or stay flat. This
calculator checks for that condition and reports plainly when a payment plan
cannot pay off the balance, rather than showing a misleadingly distant payoff
date.

## Example Calculation

A $5,000 balance at 22% APR, paid down with a fixed $250 a month, clears in
about 23 months and costs roughly $686 in interest. The same balance under a
2%-of-balance minimum payment with a $25 floor would take dramatically
longer and cost several times as much in interest — a direct, numeric
illustration of the minimum-payment trap.

## What Affects Your Payoff Time and Cost

- **Payment size relative to balance** — the single biggest factor; even a
  modest fixed payment above the minimum can cut years off the payoff time.
- **APR** — credit card rates are often far higher than other debt, so
  interest can dominate the total cost if payoff is slow.
- **New purchases** — ongoing spending on the card competes directly with
  your payment for reducing the balance.
