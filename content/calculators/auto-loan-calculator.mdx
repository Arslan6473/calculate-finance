The sticker price is only the starting point of a car loan. By the time you
sign, the amount you borrow has been shaped by your down payment, your
trade-in, any balance you still owe on that trade-in, sales tax and a stack
of title, registration and documentation fees. This calculator puts all of
those pieces together the way a dealer worksheet does, then shows the
monthly payment, the total interest and what the car really costs you over
the life of the loan.

## How to Use This Auto Loan Calculator

Enter the negotiated vehicle price before tax and fees, then the cash you
are putting down. If you are trading in a car, enter what the dealer is
paying for it and how much you still owe on its loan. Add your sales tax
rate and the total of title, registration and documentation fees. Use the
toggle to choose whether tax and fees are added to the loan or paid in cash
at signing. Finally, enter the APR you have been quoted and pick a term from
36 to 84 months.

The results show your monthly payment, the amount financed, total interest,
total cost (cash at signing plus every payment), the sales tax and any
negative equity rolled into the new loan.

## How the Amount Financed Is Calculated

```
Taxable amount   = Price − Trade-in value
Sales tax        = Taxable amount × Tax rate
Negative equity  = Amount owed on trade-in − Trade-in value (if above zero)
Amount financed  = Price − Down payment − Trade-in value
                   + Amount owed on trade-in
                   + Sales tax + Fees (when financed)
Monthly payment  = L × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)
```

In the payment formula, **L** is the amount financed, **r** is the APR
divided by 12, and **n** is the number of monthly payments. It is the same
amortization formula used for mortgages and personal loans: each payment
covers that month's interest, and the rest pays down the balance.

The calculator charges sales tax on the price minus the trade-in value.
That trade-in tax credit applies in most states, but not all. A handful
tax the full purchase price regardless of a trade-in, and some cap the
credit, so check your state's rules before relying on the tax figure.

## Example Calculation

Say you negotiate a car to $35,000, put $5,000 down, and trade in a car
the dealer values at $8,000 while you still owe $10,000 on it. Sales tax
is 7%, fees total $800, and you finance tax and fees at 7% APR for 60
months.

- Taxable amount: $35,000 − $8,000 = $27,000
- Sales tax: $27,000 × 7% = **$1,890**
- Negative equity: $10,000 − $8,000 = **$2,000**
- Amount financed: $35,000 − $5,000 − $8,000 + $10,000 + $1,890 + $800 =
  **$34,690**

At 7% for 60 months the monthly payment is **$686.90**. Over five years you
pay $41,214.00 in total, of which **$6,524.21** is interest. Add the $5,000
down payment and the total cost is **$46,214.00**.

Three changes show how much the details matter:

| Change | Monthly payment | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| As above | $686.90 | $34,690 financed |
| Pay tax and fees in cash | $633.64 | $7,690 due at signing |
| Stretch to 72 months | $591.43 | Interest rises to $7,892.96 |
| No negative equity | $488.89 | $24,690 financed |

Notice that the $2,000 of negative equity costs more than $2,000 once it
is folded into the loan, because it accrues interest for the full term on
a car you no longer own. And if your state taxed the
full $35,000 instead of $27,000, sales tax would be $2,450, an extra $560.

## What Affects Your Car Payment

- **APR** — driven by your credit score, the lender and whether the car is
  new or used. Dealer-arranged financing is not always the cheapest, so get
  a preapproval from a bank or credit union to compare.
- **Loan term** — a longer term lowers the payment but increases total
  interest, and longer loans often carry higher rates.
- **Down payment and trade-in equity** — every dollar you bring reduces the
  amount financed one-for-one and lowers the interest on top of it.
- **Negative equity** — rolling an old balance into a new loan makes it
  more likely you will owe more than the new car is worth for years.
- **Sales tax and fees** — these vary widely by state and county and can
  add several thousand dollars to the purchase.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Shopping by monthly payment.** A dealer can hit almost any payment by
  stretching the term to 72 or 84 months. Negotiate the price first, then
  compare total interest across terms.
- **Ignoring negative equity.** It does not vanish when you trade in the
  car; it moves into the new loan and grows with interest.
- **Assuming the trade-in always lowers your tax.** Most states give the
  credit, but not every one does, and some limit it.
- **Forgetting fees.** Documentation, title and registration fees are
  real costs, and financing them means paying interest on them.
- **Skipping a preapproval.** Without an outside rate to compare, it is
  hard to know whether the financing offered at the dealership is a good
  deal.
