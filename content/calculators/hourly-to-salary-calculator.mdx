An hourly wage and an annual salary describe the same thing in different
units, but they're hard to compare at a glance. This calculator converts
either one into the other and breaks the result down into monthly, biweekly,
weekly, and daily pay — accounting for the unpaid time off that a quick
"multiply by 2,080" estimate ignores.

## How to Use This Hourly to Salary Calculator

Start by choosing which figure you already know: your hourly rate or your
annual salary. Enter that amount, then the hours you typically work in a
week. Days per week only affects the daily figure, so leave it at five for a
standard Monday-to-Friday schedule. Finally, enter any weeks you take off
without pay each year. If your vacation and holidays are paid — as they are
for most salaried jobs — leave this at zero.

The results show your pay across every common period, with the figure you
didn't enter highlighted at the top.

## How the Conversion Works

The core of the conversion is the number of paid hours in a year. From
there, the two directions are simple:

```
Paid hours per year = hours per week × (52 − unpaid weeks off)
Annual salary       = hourly rate × paid hours per year
Hourly rate         = annual salary ÷ paid hours per year
```

In plain language: work out how many hours you are actually paid for in a
year, then multiply or divide by that number.

A full-time schedule of 40 hours a week for all 52 weeks is 2,080 hours,
which is where the familiar rule of thumb comes from — double the hourly
rate and add three zeros, and you're close to the annual figure. Because a
calendar year has slightly more than 52 weeks, some employers use a different
divisor: the federal government, for example, divides annual basic pay by
2,087 hours, the average number of work hours in a calendar year.

The breakdown then uses two different views of the year:

- **Weekly, biweekly, and daily** pay describe a period you actually work.
  Weekly pay is your hourly rate times your weekly hours, biweekly is two of
  those weeks, and daily is a week's pay divided by your working days.
- **Monthly** pay is your annual total averaged over twelve months, which is
  the number that matters when you're budgeting for rent or bills.

When you have no unpaid weeks off, both views agree exactly: weekly pay times
52 equals your annual salary. With unpaid weeks, the annual and monthly
figures fall while a working week's pay stays the same.

## Example Calculation

Suppose you earn $25 an hour, work 40 hours across five days, and take two
unpaid weeks off each year.

- Paid weeks: 52 − 2 = 50
- Paid hours per year: 40 × 50 = 2,000
- Annual salary: $25 × 2,000 = **$50,000**
- Monthly (average): $50,000 ÷ 12 = **$4,166.67**
- Weekly: $25 × 40 = **$1,000**
- Biweekly: 2 × $1,000 = **$2,000**
- Daily: $1,000 ÷ 5 = **$200**

The quick 2,080-hour estimate would have put your annual pay at $52,000 —
$2,000 more than you'll actually earn.

Going the other way, a $60,000 salary on a 40-hour week with no unpaid time
off works out to $60,000 ÷ 2,080 = **$28.85 an hour**, or $1,153.85 a week.

## Gross Pay, Not Take-Home Pay

Every figure here is gross pay — what you earn before federal income tax,
Social Security, Medicare, state tax, and any benefit deductions are taken
out. Your paycheck will be noticeably smaller. To see what you'll actually
take home, enter the same pay in the salary calculator, which applies the
current federal tax brackets and FICA rates.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Counting paid vacation as unpaid.** If your employer pays you for
  holidays and vacation, those weeks are still paid weeks. Only enter time
  off that doesn't come with a paycheck.
- **Comparing gross pay with take-home pay.** A salary offer is quoted before
  tax, so compare it with your gross hourly earnings, not with the net amount
  on your current pay stub.
- **Using scheduled hours instead of paid hours.** If your 8.5-hour day
  includes an unpaid 30-minute lunch, you are paid for 40 hours a week, not
  42.5.
- **Expecting overtime on a salary.** Moving from hourly to salaried work can
  change whether you're owed overtime, so a salary that matches your
  straight-time pay may be worth less if you currently work paid overtime.
- **Treating monthly pay as four weeks.** A year has 52 weeks, so the average
  month holds about 4.33 weeks of pay, and in most years two months contain
  three biweekly paydays.

## What Affects Your Conversion

- **Hours per week** — a 37.5-hour week is common in some jobs and lowers
  annual pay by about 6% compared with 40 hours at the same rate.
- **Unpaid time off** — each unpaid week removes roughly 2% of a full year's
  pay.
- **Overtime** — this calculator assumes a steady schedule at one rate. If you
  regularly work more than 40 hours and are paid overtime, the overtime
  calculator will give a more accurate annual figure.
- **Paid benefits** — health insurance, retirement matching, and paid leave
  add real value to a salaried offer that a straight hourly comparison
  doesn't capture.
