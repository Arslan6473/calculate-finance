A mortgage payment is more than principal and interest. Property tax, home
insurance, HOA fees, and — if your down payment is below 20% — private
mortgage insurance (PMI) all add up to the amount you actually pay each month.
This calculator breaks down every piece so you can see exactly where your
money goes and how much of it builds equity versus how much is interest.

## How to Use This Mortgage Calculator

Enter the home price and your planned down payment; the calculator subtracts
one from the other to find your loan amount. Choose a loan term — 10, 15, 20,
or 30 years — and enter the interest rate your lender has quoted or that
you're comparing. If you know your annual property tax and home insurance
costs, add them to see your full monthly payment rather than just principal
and interest. Add a monthly HOA fee if the property has one, and a PMI rate if
your down payment is under 20% (0.5% a year is the default, but your lender's
quote is the number to use). The extra monthly payment field is optional: use
it to see exactly how much time and interest an extra payment each month
would save.

## How Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

A fixed-rate mortgage uses an amortizing payment: the same amount is paid
every month for the life of the loan, but the split between principal and
interest shifts over time. Early payments are mostly interest, because
interest is charged on the current balance and the balance starts at its
highest point. As the balance falls, less of each payment goes to interest and
more goes to principal — which is why the last few years of a mortgage pay
down principal far faster than the first few.

## Mortgage Payment Formula

The monthly principal-and-interest payment is:

```
M = P × [ i(1 + i)^n ] / [ (1 + i)^n − 1 ]
```

- **M** is the monthly principal-and-interest payment.
- **P** is the loan amount — the home price minus your down payment.
- **i** is the monthly interest rate: the annual rate divided by 12, as a
  decimal (6.5% becomes 0.065 ÷ 12, or about 0.0054).
- **n** is the total number of monthly payments: the term in years × 12.

This calculator then adds property
tax and insurance (each divided by 12), any HOA fee, and PMI as separate
monthly costs on top. PMI is charged as your annual PMI rate × the original
loan amount ÷ 12, and the calculator walks the amortization schedule month by
month to find when your balance falls to 80% of the original home price — at
that point PMI drops out of the total.

## Example Calculation

A $400,000 home with an $80,000 down payment (20%) leaves a $320,000 loan. At
6.5% over 30 years, principal and interest come to $2,022.62 a month. Add
$4,800 a year in property tax ($400 a month) and $1,500 in insurance ($125 a
month), and the total monthly payment is $2,547.62 — with no PMI, since the
down payment already clears the 20% threshold. Over 30 years you'd pay
$408,142.36 in interest on top of the $320,000 borrowed.

Now change one thing at a time:

- **Put 10% down instead.** The loan grows to $360,000, principal and
  interest rise to $2,275.44, and PMI at 0.5% adds $150 a month. The total is
  $2,950.44. PMI lasts 95 months — just under eight years — before the
  balance reaches 80% of the price.
- **Add $200 a month in extra principal** to the original 20%-down loan. The
  loan is paid off in 281 months instead of 360, and total interest falls to
  $302,713.69 — a saving of about $105,400.
- **Choose a 15-year term** at the same 6.5% rate. Principal and interest
  rise to $2,787.54 a month, but total interest drops to $181,757.84. Freddie
  Mac's weekly rate survey reports 15-year rates below 30-year rates, so the
  real gap is often wider.

## What Affects Your Monthly Mortgage Payment?

- **Loan amount** — the payment scales in direct proportion to the amount
  borrowed.
- **Interest rate** — even a small change compounds significantly over a
  30-year term; on a $320,000 loan, moving from 6.5% to 7.5% raises the
  monthly payment from $2,022.62 to $2,237.49.
- **Loan term** — a shorter term means a higher monthly payment but far less
  total interest, because the balance is outstanding for fewer years.
- **Property tax and insurance** — these vary by location and coverage, and
  are often the most-overlooked piece of "how much house can I afford."
- **Down payment** — beyond reducing the loan amount, a down payment below
  20% typically triggers PMI, an added monthly cost that disappears once
  you've built 20% equity.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Budgeting on principal and interest alone.** Lenders quote the P&I figure,
  but your escrow payment for taxes and insurance comes out of the same
  checking account. In the example above it adds $525 a month.
- **Treating property tax as fixed.** Assessments and tax rates change, and
  insurance premiums are repriced at renewal, so your escrow payment is
  recalculated over time. Leave room in your budget for increases.
- **Forgetting PMI on a low down payment.** At 10% down in the example, PMI
  costs $150 a month for nearly eight years. Ask your lender for the actual
  PMI rate rather than relying on the default.
- **Comparing loans with different terms by monthly payment only.** A 30-year
  loan always looks cheaper per month than a 15-year loan; compare total
  interest as well, which this calculator shows alongside the payment.
- **Assuming extra payments go to principal automatically.** Some servicers
  apply extra money to the next scheduled payment unless you tell them
  otherwise. Confirm it is credited to principal, or the savings shown here
  won't materialize.
