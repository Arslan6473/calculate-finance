Take-home pay is what's left after federal income tax, Social Security,
Medicare, and any state or local tax — and it's usually meaningfully less
than the salary on an offer letter. This calculator applies the real 2026 US
federal tax brackets and FICA rates to give an accurate breakdown, rather than
a single flat-percentage estimate.

## How to Use This Salary Calculator

Enter your pay as an annual salary, a monthly amount, or an hourly rate — if
hourly, also enter your typical hours per week. Choose how often you're
actually paid (weekly, every two weeks, twice a month, or monthly) and your
filing status, since both change the numbers. Enter your combined state and
local tax rate as a flat percentage; add any annual pre-tax deductions (like
a traditional 401(k)) and post-tax deductions (like a Roth 401(k)) if they
apply to you. The results show your take-home pay per year, per month, and
per paycheck, along with each tax and your effective and marginal rates.

## How Take-Home Pay Is Calculated

Federal income tax is calculated using the actual 2026 progressive tax
brackets for your filing status, after subtracting pre-tax deductions and the
standard deduction from your gross income. Social Security is 6.2% of wages
up to the annual wage base, and Medicare is 1.45% of all wages, with an
additional 0.9% surtax above a filing-status threshold — both calculated on
your full gross wages, since 401(k) contributions reduce income tax but not
FICA. State and local tax is applied as the flat rate you provide, to income
after pre-tax deductions, since it varies too much by location to compute
precisely without knowing your state.

## Take-Home Pay Formula

```
Taxable income = Gross − Pre-tax deductions − Standard deduction
Federal tax    = Σ (income within each bracket × that bracket's rate)
FICA           = 6.2% × wages up to $184,500 + 1.45% × all wages
                 + 0.9% × wages above the Additional Medicare threshold
State tax      = State rate × (Gross − Pre-tax deductions)
Take-home      = Gross − Federal tax − FICA − State tax
                 − Pre-tax deductions − Post-tax deductions
```

- **Gross** is your annual pay before anything is taken out. Hourly pay is
  converted as rate × hours per week × 52.
- **Standard deduction** for 2026 is $16,100 for single filers and married
  filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for
  heads of household.
- **Σ** means "add up": each slice of taxable income is taxed only at the
  rate of the bracket it falls in.
- The Additional Medicare threshold is $200,000 for single filers and heads
  of household, $250,000 for married filing jointly, and $125,000 for
  married filing separately.

## Marginal Rate vs. Effective Rate

Your marginal tax rate is what you pay on your next dollar of income — the
rate of your highest federal bracket. Your federal effective rate is your
income tax divided by your gross income, and it's always lower than your
marginal rate, because every dollar below your top bracket is taxed at a
lower rate. This calculator shows both, but its effective rate includes FICA
and state tax too, so it reflects everything taken for taxes — which is why
it can end up above the marginal federal rate, as in the example below.

## Example Calculation

A single filer earning $85,000 a year, paid every two weeks, with a 5% state
tax rate and no deductions:

- Taxable income is $68,900 after the $16,100 standard deduction.
- Federal tax is $9,870: $1,240 at 10%, $4,560 at 12%, and $4,070 at 22%.
- Social Security is $5,270 and Medicare is $1,232.50.
- State tax is $4,250.

Total tax is $20,622.50, leaving $64,377.50 a year — $2,476.06 per biweekly
paycheck, or 75.74% of gross. The effective tax rate is 24.26%; the marginal
federal rate is 22%.

Now add a $6,000 traditional 401(k) contribution. Federal tax falls to $8,550
and state tax to $3,950, while FICA is unchanged. Take-home drops to
$59,997.50 — only $4,380 less, even though $6,000 went into savings.

## What Affects Your Take-Home Pay

- **Filing status** — different brackets and a different standard deduction
  apply to single, married, and head-of-household filers.
- **Pre-tax deductions** — reduce your federal (and state) taxable income,
  but not the wages FICA is calculated on.
- **State and local tax** — varies enormously by location; a handful of
  states charge none at all.
- **Income level** — higher earners cross into higher brackets, stop paying
  Social Security tax above the wage base, and eventually owe the additional
  Medicare surtax.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Thinking a raise into a higher bracket lowers take-home pay.** Only the
  income above the bracket threshold is taxed at the higher rate, so bracket
  math alone never makes a raise shrink your after-tax pay.
- **Using your marginal rate to estimate total tax.** In the example, the
  22% bracket applies to just $18,500 of income; the effective rate is what
  determines your paycheck.
- **Assuming 401(k) contributions avoid all taxes.** Traditional 401(k)
  deferrals reduce income tax but are still subject to Social Security and
  Medicare tax.
- **Ignoring unpaid time off on hourly pay.** The calculator multiplies
  your hourly rate by 52 weeks. If you take unpaid weeks off, your real
  annual gross is lower.
- **Expecting your paycheck to match exactly.** Employers withhold using
  IRS withholding tables and your W-4, and benefits such as health premiums
  vary. This is an estimate of annual liability, not a pay stub.
